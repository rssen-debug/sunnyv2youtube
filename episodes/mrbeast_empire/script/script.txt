###A1
On June twelfth, 2026, a record quietly fell on YouTube. For the first time ever, a single person passed half a billion subscribers. Not a company... not a TV network. One guy, from a small town in North Carolina. And here's the strange part: he says he's broke. So how does the biggest channel in human history burn through half a billion dollars? This is MrBeast.

###A2
His real name is Jimmy Donaldson. He grew up in Greenville, North Carolina, and at thirteen, he started uploading videos almost nobody watched. For five years, he and a small group of friends studied exactly one thing, every single day... the YouTube algorithm. What makes you click. What makes you stay. And then, in January 2017, after a thousand failed uploads, he finally cracked it.

###A3
He filmed himself counting to one hundred thousand. Forty hours, on camera... and the internet watched. Within days it exploded, and Jimmy understood the formula instantly: absurd challenge, total commitment, bigger stakes. He buried himself alive for fifty hours. He handed strangers life-changing money on camera, because videos that cost more, made more. And then he went further than anyone thought possible.

###A4
He rebuilt Squid Game in real life, full sets, four hundred fifty-six thousand dollars on the line... and hundreds of millions of people watched. He launched Feastables, a chocolate brand, into supermarkets worldwide. And then came the biggest bet of all: Beast Games. A reported hundred-million-dollar deal with Amazon. A private city, built just for contestants. A ten-million-dollar grand prize. Look at this.

###A5
But in the summer of 2024... it cracked. A longtime co-host faced disturbing accusations and stepped away; a law firm his company hired later called the key claims unsubstantiated. Weeks after that, former contestants filed a class action against him and Amazon, alleging unsafe conditions, unpaid wages, and harassment on Beast Games. The headlines that built him... turned on him. And for the first time, the question wasn't how big he could get. It was whether it would all survive.

###B1
His answer was to go bigger. A new CEO, Jeff Housenbold, came in to turn a YouTube channel into a company. Eighty-five million dollars built Beast Land, a pop-up theme park in Saudi Arabia, and it turned a profit in forty-five days. And the lawsuit? Still moving through court, unresolved. And the audience? It just kept growing.

###B2
Fast forward to right now. January 2026: Beast Games, season two. Two hundred contestants. Strong versus smart. A five-million-dollar prize... and another record launch for Prime Video. Behind the scenes, Beast Industries is now valued at five point two billion dollars, with seven hundred and fifty employees, on a hundred-and-thirty-two-acre campus. And on June twelfth, the channel passed five hundred million subscribers, and just kept counting. A two-point-six-billion-dollar fortune on paper... and he still says he's broke. He said it himself.

###A6
Which brings us back to the strange part. Half a billion subscribers. One point three billion people watching, every quarter... and a borrowed bank account. The greatest stunt of MrBeast's life was never getting buried alive, or buying an island. It's turning the biggest audience ever assembled into something the algorithm can't take away. He's still counting... and he's nowhere near done. Subscribe, and watch what comes next.
